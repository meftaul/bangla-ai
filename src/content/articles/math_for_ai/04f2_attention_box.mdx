Som's first question was about the library's machine B itself. Cosine has a cost. On every search, measure every book's length, take the root, divide. What if the library has a million books?

Apu's brother had an idea. When a book goes on the shelf, make its card's length 1 once and for all, as in 3.6. Then run the three measures and see.

<MillionBooks story />

<OneRanking />

<Then>

Three measures, one order. A card 1 unit long leaves nothing to divide — both lengths are 1. So the plain box is the cosine:

$$\cos\theta = \hat{u} \cdot \hat{v} \qquad (\text{when both are 1 unit long})$$

<HatFormula />

That is exactly what real search systems do. Every document is normalised once and stored; then each search runs only the box — the cheapest, fastest job a computer does. A GPU is really a machine for running millions upon millions of boxes at once.

<Then>

Why did distance give the same order? For two arrows 1 unit long, distance and cosine are tied together:

$$\|\hat{u} - \hat{v}\|^2 = 2 - 2\cos\theta$$

<ChordCos />

The bigger the cosine, the smaller the distance. So after normalising, “closest” and “most the same direction” are the same sentence. That's why many libraries give a “cosine distance”, $1 - \cos\theta$, where 0 means exactly identical.

Som was right. Inside ChatGPT really is the haat's box, called attention. The library's search runs on the box, the distance sum runs on the box, the neuron runs on the box. If you bet on “true”, you won.

<Then>

Across four articles we picked up this formula's pieces one at a time. Turning a thing into a list of numbers, seeing that list as an arrow, measuring the arrow's length, and finally how much two arrows point the same way. The whole thing in one sentence:

**To know how alike two things are, make their arrows, see how much they point the same way, and divide the lengths away so only the direction is left.**

<FourArticles />

A search engine matches pages to your question this way. A film app finds films like the ones you like this way. A chatbot finds the paragraph that holds your answer this way. All three, you can now do by hand.

<Step>

In 4.3, Mama's recipe was $v \cdot w = \|v\| \times \|w\| \times \cos\theta$. On the left, the box's number. On the right, length, length and angle.

Think of the two sides as the two pans of a balance. The job: get $\cos\theta$ alone, without tipping the balance.

<RunBackwards />

<Then>

Divide both sides by the same thing and the balance stays level. Divide by the two lengths, and only $\cos\theta$ is left on the right:

$$\cos\theta = \frac{v \cdot w}{\|v\| \, \|w\|}$$

Say it out loud: **take the box's number, divide by the two lengths. What is left is only how much the two arrows point the same way.** It's called **cosine similarity**. The same box, with the loudness shaved off.

<ShaveLengths />

Its real power shows up with more slots. In two slots you can measure the angle with a protractor. But the words in Som's phone have 300 slots — no protractor there, and no picture either. Yet the formula works just fine. So in a world of many slots, “two words are 20° apart” simply means: this is the formula's answer.

<NoProtractor />

</Then>

</Step>

<Step>

Cosine similarity has a big advantage. Whether the box's 22 is big or small depends on the lengths — you can't say on its own. But cosine always sits on a fixed scale, like the roof's stick: −1 to 1.

Turn the angle between two arrows and get to know the scale.

<ScaleCard />

<Then>

The scale goes like this: 1 means exactly the same direction, 0 means a right angle — no match at all — and −1 means exactly opposite. The numbers in between are not evenly spread. 0.9 sounds like “90% match”, but it's really 26° apart. And 0.5 doesn't mean “half a match”, it means 60° apart. So don't read cosine as a percent. Read who comes first, who after, and roughly how close.

<UnevenScale />

Why does cosine never go below −1 or above 1? You saw it on the roof: a shadow is never longer than its stick. Books give this a grand name, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. The name is heavy; the reason is one line.

## Try it

<Check
  question="Samin likes (1, 3). In hand, two films: b = (4, 2) and c = (2, 3). Going by cosine similarity, which do you give?"
  options={["b, angle 45°", "c, angle about 15°", "Both fit the same"]}
  answer={1}
  hint="First two boxes, then divide each by the two lengths. Samin's length is √10."
>

Boxes: $(1, 3) \cdot b = 4 + 6 = 10$, and $(1, 3) \cdot c = 2 + 9 = 11$. Lengths: $\sqrt{10}$, $\sqrt{20}$, $\sqrt{13}$. So $\cos(b) = 10 / \sqrt{200} \approx 0.707$, that is 45°, and $\cos(c) = 11 / \sqrt{130} \approx 0.965$, about 15°. Give $c$.

Notice: with the box, 10 against 11 — nearly a tie. With cosine, 0.707 against 0.965 — a clear gap.

Next morning, the rest of the haat's goods — sacks of paddy and new pots and pans — were loaded onto the boat. The river's current runs the wrong way, and there's no wind, so the sail is no use. Majhi chacha and his brother stepped onto the bank, tied a long rope to the boat's mast, and started walking along the bank, pulling. This is called towing.

Fahim saw the rope was long, some twenty arm-lengths. He asked, and Majhi chacha said, “The longer the rope, the less of the pull is wasted.” They must reach the ghat before evening; there's no time to waste pull.

<Step>

Say the river runs straight east. Any pull on the boat can be split into two parts. One goes forward along the river, and one at a right angle to the river, towards the bank.

Drag the pull around and watch how the two parts change.

<SplitPull />

<Then>

The green part is the pull's shadow on the river — 4.3's projection. The yellow part is what's left over, at a right angle to the river. Join the two and the whole pull comes back:

$$w = (\text{the shadow along the river}) + (\text{the rest, at a right angle})$$

<ShadowDrop />

With the river going east, it's easy. The shadow is the pull's first number, the rest the second. In 4.4 you saw it: a shadow on an axis is that slot's number. But a river doesn't always run east.

<Step>

Soon the river will bend, and finding the shadow's head will take a test. Try the test once first.

<Check

2 × (−1) + 1 × 2 = −2 + 2 = 0. With the box, 0 means a right angle, however many dimensions.

</Check>

<Step>

Ahead, the river has taken a bend and now runs along $v = (2, 3)$. The rope's pull is $w = (2, 1)$. Where is the shadow's head?

Slide a point along the river and find the spot where the line down from the rope's tip meets the river at a right angle. You remember what a right angle means for the box? 0.

<RiverBend story />

<FindFoot />

<Then>

The shadow's head landed exactly 7/13 of the way along the river's arrow. 7 and 13 — do those two numbers look familiar? $w \cdot v = 4 + 3 = 7$, and $v \cdot v = 4 + 9 = 13$. No coincidence.

<SevenThirteen />

From 4.3 we know the shadow's length is $\|w\| \cos\theta$, and the box is $w \cdot v = \|v\| \, \|w\| \cos\theta$. So the shadow's length is $w \cdot v \div \|v\|$. To find what share of the river's arrow that is, divide by $\|v\|$ once more: $w \cdot v \div \|v\|^2$. And in 4.1 you saw $\|v\|^2 = v \cdot v$. So the share is $7 \div 13$.

<WhySeven />

</Then>

</Step>

<Step>

So the whole sum can be laid out as one machine. Run it one step at a time.

<ShadowRecipe />

<Then>

Written as an arrow, the shadow is:

$$\text{shadow} = \frac{w \cdot v}{v \cdot v} \, v$$

Say it out loud: two boxes, one division, then stretch the river's arrow by that share. No root needed, and no protractor either.

<RecipeAloud />

And the last line is the real one. Run the box between the leftover and the river and you get 0 — a perfect right angle. Not just for this rope: for any pull and any river. So any vector can be split into two pieces with respect to any direction:

$$w = \underbrace{\text{the shadow}}_{\text{along } v} + \underbrace{\text{the rest}}_{\text{at a right angle to } v}$$

<Step>

The river is straight again. But where does the rest go — the pull towards the bank? At the stern sits the helmsman, hand on the tiller. What happens if he lets go?

<RudderMan story />

<SidePull />

<Then>

The moment the tiller was let go, the boat began to drift towards the bank. The yellow part doesn't move the boat forward one step; it only pulls sideways, and the helmsman holds it off with the tiller the whole time. That is the “wasted pull” Majhi chacha was talking about.

<RudderHolds />

Remember Mami in 4.2? She pushed at a right angle, and the van didn't move a hair. A box of 0 means exactly this: **there is nothing of one in the direction of the other.** However far one goes, it tells you nothing about the other.

<ZeroNotMinus />

So a cosine of 0 means “no match”, not “opposite”. Opposite means −1, and being opposite is a strong relationship too. If someone always says the opposite of what you say, you can say your opinion for them just by hearing theirs. 0 means there is no relationship at all.

</Then>

<Step>

Now the bet is settled. The bank is 3 metres from the boat. The boatmen pull with a total strength of about 10, short rope or long.

Make the rope shorter and longer, and watch how much of that 10 goes forward.

<LongRope />

<Then>

With a short rope the boatmen stand almost level with the boat, the rope stands steep, and a big share of the pull goes towards the bank. With a long rope they are far ahead, the angle shrinks, and $\cos\theta$ comes close to 1. Then almost the whole pull goes forward. **Majhi chacha was right.**

<RopeFan />

One honest limit, though. A very long rope is heavy, sags into the water, and is hard to handle at the bend. So the boatmen pick a middle length, and tie the rope at the top of the mast so it doesn't snag on the bank's bushes. The maths says: longer is better. How long — that, reality decides.

<Step>

Before landing at the ghat, an idea struck Fahim. Taking a shadow turns an arrow into one number: how far along the river. What if a whole pile of points lies roughly along one line? Take everyone's shadow on that line — what do you lose?

<FahimIdea story />

<KeepShadows />

<Then>

Taking shadows on the line of the spread, each point came down from two numbers to one, and lost only a small leftover, at most 0.4. But taking shadows on the line at a right angle, they all piled into one tiny spot, and lost almost everything.

<OnePoint />

This is the heart of a famous trick called **PCA** (principal component analysis). Say each point of your data is 300 numbers, but really it spreads along only a few directions. Take shadows on those few directions and throw the rest away. The work of 300 numbers gets done by a few, and only small leftovers are lost. How to find the best directions is much further ahead in this series.

## Try it

<Check
  question="Mami said, “A cosine of 0 means the two point exactly opposite.” Is she right?"
  options={["Yes, 0 means opposite", "No, 0 means a right angle, no relationship at all; opposite is −1", "No, 0 means the same direction"]}
  answer={1}
  hint="When the helmsman let go of the tiller, did the bankwards pull take the boat backwards, or did it do nothing forwards or backwards?"
>

$\cos 90^\circ = 0$: a right angle. The bankwards pull took the boat neither forward nor backward. Nothing of one is in the direction of the other — no relationship.

Opposite is $\cos 180^\circ = -1$, and that is a strong relationship. In 4.2, Rafik was hurting the van precisely because he pushed leaning backwards. Mami has mixed up 0 and −1.

</Check>

<Step>

## The end! The boat reached the ghat

Majhi chacha said: the longer the rope, the less pull is wasted. The claim held. A long rope makes a small angle, and at a small angle the shadow is nearly the whole pull. If you bet on the first one, you won. And we found where the wasted pull goes: at a right angle to the river, towards the bank.

In one line: **any arrow can be split into two pieces with respect to any direction — its shadow along that direction, and the rest at a right angle.**

<Step>

2 across and 4 up. But the fence isn't 2 columns away. It's 6 columns past the scarecrow. So what does the road do in just one column?

<OneColumn />

<Then>

Each column Shiku stepped across, the road went up 2. Never 4, never 1. Always 2.

So “4 up for 2 across” really meant two columns of 2 each. A straight road goes up the same amount for every one step across, and that amount has a name: the **slope**. It's up ÷ across, so here 4 ÷ 2 = 2.

<Step>

Nasib doesn't believe it.

<AnyTwoDots />

<Then>

Every pair gave 2. A far-apart pair made a big triangle and a close pair a small one, but they're all the same shape. Go twice as far across and you go twice as far up.

So there's nothing special about the box and the scarecrow. Any two dots on a straight road give you its slope. **Nasib's objection didn't survive**, and Samin's two dots are enough for Nana's fence.

<Then>

4 is more than 3, so Nasib's call isn't silly. But Rina's bee needed 2 columns for its 4. In one column it climbed only 2, and Karim's climbed 3. So Karim's road is the steeper one. **That's Nasib's second miss.**

That's why the slope is the up for *one* across, not just the up. A bigger slope means a steeper road.

## Your turn: Nana's fence

Now the fence. It's at column 9, three columns past the edge of Samin's page. Nana is holding the dish. Which row does it go on?

<FenceTurn />

<Then>

You never saw the fence on paper, and you didn't need to. Start from a dot you know, count the columns to where you're going, and climb 2 for each one.

The bee's road does only one thing: 2 up for every 1 across. Know that one number and you can follow the road anywhere.

Then count from the dot you know. The second dot sits at column 1, and the pond's edge is at column 3. That's 2 columns, 3 up each, so 6 up on top of row 4. Anyone who tapped row 7 counted only one column.

## The end! Nana's dish is on the fence

<DishOnFence story />

Row 17. The bee came straight to the dish. Nasib's row 9 would have missed it by 8 rows.

It all came from two dots. From the box to the scarecrow was 2 across and 4 up, so the road climbs 2 for every 1 across. That's its **slope**, and with it you can follow a straight road right off the page.

> Slope = up ÷ across. Any two dots on a straight road give the same answer.

Next morning, three bees fly out side by side, perfectly parallel. All three roads have slope 2. Nasib says that makes them the same road. Samin's page shows three different roads. So what tells them apart? That's the next journey.
